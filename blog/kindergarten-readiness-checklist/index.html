Kindergarten readiness checklist

By the FirstFoundationkids team · November 6, 2026

Starting school is a big step. This checklist covers the skills many children are working on before kindergarten (called Reception or Year 1 in the UK, and Foundation or Prep in Australia), with easy ideas to practice at home.

An important note first: no child needs to tick every box. Schools expect children to arrive with a wide range of skills, and teachers are there to help them grow. Use this list as a guide for playful practice, not as a test.

Early reading and language

  • Recognizes some letters, especially the ones in their name.
  • Knows that print is read from left to right and top to bottom.
  • Can hear rhymes (cat, hat) and first sounds in words.
  • Listens to a short story and answers simple questions about it.
  • Speaks in full sentences and can tell you about their day.

Practice at home: read together every day, ask “What do you think happens next?”, and play rhyming and “I spy” games.

Early math

  • Counts to 10 or higher, and counts small groups of objects correctly.
  • Recognizes numbers 1 to 10.
  • Names basic shapes like circle, square and triangle.
  • Understands “more”, “fewer”, “bigger” and “smaller”.
  • Spots and continues simple patterns (red, blue, red, blue…).

Practice at home: count snacks and stairs, find shapes on a walk, and make patterns with blocks or beads. See our guide on teaching counting.

Fine motor skills

  • Holds a crayon or pencil with a comfortable grip.
  • Draws lines, circles and simple pictures.
  • Is starting to write their first name.
  • Uses child-safe scissors with help.

Practice at home: play dough, threading beads, coloring pages and tracing letters all build hand strength.

Social and emotional skills

  • Can name feelings like happy, sad, angry and scared.
  • Takes turns and shares, with reminders.
  • Follows simple two-step instructions.
  • Can be apart from a parent for a few hours.

Practice at home: play board games for turn-taking, talk about feelings in stories, and give simple jobs like “Put on your shoes and get your bag.”

Self-care

  • Uses the toilet and washes hands on their own.
  • Puts on a coat and shoes (maybe not laces yet).
  • Opens a lunch box and snack packets.
  • Knows their full name.

Keep it positive

The most useful thing you can give a child before school is a love of learning. Short, cheerful practice, lots of reading together and plenty of praise for effort go a long way.
