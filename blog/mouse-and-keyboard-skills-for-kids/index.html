First computer skills: teaching mouse and keyboard use

By the FirstFoundationkids team · November 6, 2026

Many children learn to tap a tablet before they can talk in full sentences, but a mouse and a keyboard are new skills. Here is a gentle step-by-step plan.

Why mouse and keyboard skills still matter

Tablets are easy for small hands, but at school, in libraries and later at work, children will meet laptops and desktop computers. Using a mouse also builds hand-eye coordination, and finding letters on a keyboard is great letter recognition practice.

Step 1: Holding the mouse

Show your child how to rest their hand on the mouse, with the pointer finger on the left button. A small mouse that fits a child’s hand makes this much easier. Let them move it around and watch the pointer move on the screen.

Step 2: Pointing

Ask your child to move the pointer onto big, colorful targets. Many children are surprised that moving the mouse up the table moves the pointer up the screen. Give them time to explore.

Step 3: Clicking

Clicking without moving the mouse is harder than it looks. Practice with big targets first, like popping large bubbles, then make them smaller over time.

Step 4: Click and drag

Dragging means holding the button down while moving. It is a tricky skill for little fingers, so start with short distances and large objects.

Step 5: The keyboard

  • Start with the letters in your child’s name.
  • Show the space bar (the long one) and the Enter key.
  • Point out that keyboard letters are capitals, but they type lowercase letters on the screen. This is a good chance to practice matching capitals and lowercase.
  • Later, try typing short words like “cat” or “sun”.

Tips for success

  • Keep sessions short. Five to ten minutes is plenty for young children.
  • Sit together at first and celebrate small wins.
  • Adjust the chair height so your child’s arm rests comfortably on the table.
  • For left-handed children, you can switch the mouse buttons in the computer settings.
